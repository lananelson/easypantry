## Ingredients

- 1 grapefruit
- 1 orange
- 1 lemon
- 2 medium fennel bulbs
- 2 tablespoons harissa paste, plus more
- 1½ teaspoons toasted sesame oil
- 2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
- Kosher salt
- 4 (6-ounce) salmon fillets

## Instructions

1. In a large bowl, whisk together 2 tablespoons harissa, 1½ teaspoons sesame oil, 2 tablespoons olive oil, and ½ teaspoon salt until incorporated.
2. Cut away the peel and any white pith from the grapefruit and orange. Remove the segments from between the membranes and tear them in half into irregular 1-inch pieces. Add them to the harissa vinaigrette.
3. Cut the lemon in half and squeeze the juice from one half into the bowl, stirring to combine. Cut the remaining half into wedges for serving.
4. Trim and discard the stalks and fronds from the fennel bulbs. Quarter each bulb through the root end, then slice lengthwise as thinly as possible. Add the sliced fennel to the bowl but do not toss with the vinaigrette yet. Chill until ready to serve.
5. Pat the salmon fillets dry with paper towels and season them all over with 2 teaspoons salt. Place each fillet skin-side down in a large, dry nonstick skillet.
6. Set the skillet over medium heat and cook, undisturbed, pressing firmly on each fillet so the skin stays in contact with the pan, until the skin is very crispy and releases easily, 10 to 12 minutes.
7. Flip the salmon and cook until it is nearly cooked through, about 1 minute more. The sides should appear mostly opaque with a small area of translucency in the middle.
8. Transfer each fillet to a plate, skin-side up.
9. Toss the fennel and citrus a few times to coat them evenly in the dressing. Taste and add another tablespoon or two of harissa if you want more heat.
10. Pile the salad alongside the salmon, drizzle any extra vinaigrette over the top, and serve with the lemon wedges.

## Notes

- Molly's key technique for especially crisp salmon skin is to start the fish in a cold, dry nonstick skillet rather than dropping it into a hot oiled pan.
- Harissa can vary a lot in heat, so taste yours before adding more.
- Wait until just before serving to toss the fennel with the acidic, salty vinaigrette so it does not wilt prematurely.
- Drying the salmon thoroughly matters because surface moisture creates steam and makes the skin soggy.
- No extra oil is needed in the skillet; the salmon skin releases enough fat as it cooks.
