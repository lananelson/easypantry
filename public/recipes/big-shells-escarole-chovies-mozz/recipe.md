## Ingredients

- 1 large escarole or radicchio or large bunch of kale
- 8 garlic cloves
- 1 tablespoon oregano or thyme leaves
- 8 ounces fresh whole-milk mozzarella
- 1/2 cup heavy cream
- 2 ounces grated Parmesan cheese (about 1/2 cup)
- Kosher salt
- 1/4 cup plus 2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
- 3/4 cup panko bread crumbs
- 5 oil-packed anchovy fillets
- 1/2 teaspoon red pepper flakes
- 12 ounces dried large pasta shells

## Instructions

1. Bring a large pot of heavily salted water to a boil.
2. Trim the stem of the escarole. Cut or tear the leaves into roughly 2-inch pieces, wash them, and dry thoroughly. Thinly slice the garlic. Tear the mozzarella into 1/2-inch pieces.
3. Heat 2 tablespoons olive oil in a large Dutch oven over medium-low heat. Add the panko and cook, stirring often so it toasts evenly, until golden brown, 6 to 8 minutes. Transfer to a small bowl, season with salt, and wipe out any crumbs left behind.
4. Return the Dutch oven to medium heat. Add 1/4 cup olive oil, the garlic, and the anchovies and cook, stirring often, until the garlic is softened but not browned, about 3 minutes.
5. Add the escarole and red pepper flakes, stirring once or twice to coat the escarole in the oil. Cover and cook until the escarole wilts, about 2 minutes. Uncover and continue cooking until any released water has cooked off, about 5 minutes; the bottom of the pot should be relatively dry.
6. Add the heavy cream, bring the sauce to a bare simmer over medium heat, and simmer until the cream thickens slightly, about 2 minutes.
7. Working with one small handful at a time, add the grated Parmesan to the sauce, stirring between additions. Keep stirring until all the cheese has melted and the sauce is smooth. Season with salt. Remove from the heat and cover.
8. Once the water boils, add the large shells and stir once or twice. Cook until al dente according to the package directions. Scoop out and reserve 1 cup of the pasta water, then drain.
9. Return the Dutch oven to medium heat and add the cooked shells and 1/2 cup of the reserved pasta water. Cook, stirring frequently so all the nooks and crannies are coated, until the sauce thickens again and clings to the pasta, 2 to 3 minutes.
10. Stir in the oregano. Remove the pot from the heat and add the torn mozzarella a few handfuls at a time, stirring between additions so the cheese distributes throughout the pasta in pockets. Taste and adjust the seasoning as needed.
11. Scatter about half of the toasted panko generously over the pasta and serve with more panko alongside.

## Notes

- Molly originally planned this as a baked pasta casserole, but after testing decided the stovetop version gave the same creamy, cheesy effect with far less work.
- The toasted panko is meant to provide the crunchy contrast that baked pasta would normally get from the oven.
