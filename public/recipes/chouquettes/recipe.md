## Ingredients

- 1 stick unsalted butter
- 1 cup water
- 1 tablespoon sugar
- 1/8 teaspoon salt
- 1 cup flour
- 3 eggs
- 1 to 2 tablespoons milk, half and half, or heavy cream
- 2 to 4 tablespoons pearl sugar

## Instructions

1. Preheat the oven to 400°F.
2. To make choux pastry, place the butter, water, sugar, and salt in a saucepan, cover, and bring to a boil. Take the pan off the heat when it boils, and dump in the flour. Stir to incorporate with a wooden spoon. Return the pan to medium-low heat, and stir constantly for about 30 seconds, until the dough comes away from the pan. Scoop the dough out of the pan and into a bowl to cool down for just a couple minutes. Put the pan to soak right away. Then add one egg at a time, stirring vigorously with the wooden spoon. When all three eggs are absorbed, you'll have a thick, smooth, and sticky dough.
3. Use two tablespoons to drop tablespoon-sized mounds of choux pastry onto a Silpat-lined baking sheet. Brush lightly with the milk/half and half/cream, and sprinkle with pearl sugar. They puff up a lot, so they can handle more sugar than you think.
4. Bake for 10 minutes at 400°F. Lower the heat to 350°F, and bake for 35 to 40 minutes, until the chouquettes are puffed, golden, and hard to the touch. Cool on a rack. Eat them as is, or drizzle with honey or smother with jam.

## Notes

- Chouquettes are profiterole shells crusted in pearl sugar, sold in baskets atop pastry cases in French bakeries. Once you've mastered choux, you can make beignets, gougères, éclairs, and profiteroles too.
