## Ingredients

- 4 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs and 4 chicken drumsticks (or equivalent chicken parts)
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 4 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil, or more as needed
- About 1 pound Italian sausages (sweet or spicy), cut into chunks
- 2 large shallots, minced
- 1 (8-ounce) jar pickled sweet or hot cherry peppers with the vinegar
- 6 garlic cloves, crushed
- 2 cups dry white wine
- 1 quart chicken broth
- 2 tablespoons fresh lemon juice, plus additional lemon halves
- 1 teaspoon red pepper flakes (optional)
- 4 tablespoons (1/2 stick) unsalted butter
- 1/4 cup chopped fresh flat-leaf parsley
- Crusty bread, roasted potatoes, or pasta, for serving

## Instructions

1. Season the chicken pieces with salt and pepper on both sides.
2. In a large skillet or Dutch oven, heat the olive oil over medium-high heat. Add the chicken pieces and brown them, 4 to 5 minutes per side. Remove and set aside.
3. In the same skillet, brown the sausage pieces. Remove and set them aside with the chicken.
4. Reduce the heat to medium, adding more olive oil if needed. Add the shallots and pickled peppers, reserving the vinegar. Sauté until the shallots are translucent and the peppers are softened.
5. Add the garlic and sauté for another 1 to 2 minutes.
6. Pour in the white wine and reserved pepper vinegar to deglaze the pan, scraping up the browned bits from the bottom. Let it simmer and reduce by half.
7. Return the chicken and sausage to the skillet. Add the chicken broth, lemon juice, and red pepper flakes, if using.
8. Let everything simmer until the chicken is cooked through and the sauce has thickened, 20 to 25 minutes.
9. Whisk the butter into the sauce, then add the parsley and a fresh squeeze of lemon.
10. Serve with crusty bread, roasted potatoes, or pasta to soak up the sauce.

## Notes

- Christian Petroni describes Chicken Scarpariello as a red-sauce Italian-American dish especially associated with the Tri-State area.
- The sauce gets acidity from three sources: the cherry-pepper pickling liquid, white wine, and lemon juice.
- The book notes that the same preparation works well with pork.
