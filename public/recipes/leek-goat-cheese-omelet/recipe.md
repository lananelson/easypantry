## Ingredients

- 1 to 3 leeks
- Olive oil
- Kosher salt
- 2 to 3 eggs (no more than 3 for an 8-inch skillet)
- Freshly cracked pepper
- 1 heaping tsp. butter (regular or clarified)
- Goat cheese, crumbled
- 1 lb. unsalted butter (optional, for clarified butter)

## Instructions

1. Sweat the leeks: Slice off the ends of the leek and discard. Slice the leek in half lengthwise, then crosswise into thin half moons. Place in a bowl of cold water and break up the slices with your fingers. Let sit a couple of minutes so any dirt falls to the bottom, then scoop them out into a colander to drain.
2. Place a medium sauté pan over medium heat and add a couple teaspoons of olive oil. Pat the leeks dry (a little wet is okay), add them to the pan and cook until softened. Add a pinch of kosher salt and continue to sauté until just beginning to brown, about 15 minutes. Transfer to a bowl next to the stove.
3. Beat the eggs with a fork or whisk until yolks and whites are evenly mixed — they should stream down evenly when you lift the fork. Season with a pinch of salt and pepper.
4. Preheat the oven to 350ºF, just in case. Line up your ingredients next to the stovetop: butter, salt and pepper, leeks, crumbled goat cheese.
5. Heat an 8- or 9-inch nonstick skillet over high heat (2-3 minutes on an electric stove). Add the butter. It should sizzle gently and skid around the pan. If it doesn't, the pan isn't hot enough; if it browns instantly, it's too hot — wipe it out, let the pan cool a bit, and start over.
6. Tilt the pan so the butter coats evenly, then pour the eggs into the center, scraping out the bowl. Let them sit 5 to 10 seconds, gently swirling the pan.
7. When the rim of the eggs lightens in color, starting at 12 o'clock, drag the eggs to the center, letting uncooked egg fill the space behind. Repeat at 6 o'clock, then 9, then 3. Keep repeating from different spots until you can no longer move the eggs, about 1 minute total. Turn off the heat, remove the pan, and sprinkle leeks and goat cheese over top.
8. The eggs should look a little soft on top. If too unset for your liking, pop the pan in the oven for about a minute, no longer.
9. Run a spatula around the edges. Starting at one end, fold the edge into the center, rolling the omelet into a spiral, and ease it onto the plate.
10. Clarified butter (optional): Place butter in a small pot over low heat and bring to a simmer. Simmer until the foam rises to the top. Skim off foam and discard (or save for oatmeal, bread, polenta, popcorn). Store in a mason jar for months — at room temperature in winter, in the fridge in a warmer climate.

## Notes

- Any member of the onion family can replace the leeks. Fontina, Cheddar, or Gruyère can replace the goat cheese.
- Clarified butter helps prevent browning but regular butter works fine.
- Extra sautéed leeks keep in the fridge at least a week and are delicious in pasta.
