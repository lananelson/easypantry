## Ingredients

- 1 lb (450 g) ground pork, 20–30% fat
- 3 lb (1350 g) baby or regular bok choy
- 2 tbsp soy sauce
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt (1 tsp if Diamond Crystal)
- 1 1/2 tsp sugar
- 3 dashes white pepper
- 650 g square wonton wrappers, 3 inch (about 90–100)
- Neutral oil
- Soy sauce, rice vinegar, and Chinese chili oil, for dipping
- Chicken stock and sliced scallions, to serve in broth (optional)

## Instructions

1. Heat a large wok or heavy pot over medium to medium-high heat with 1 tbsp oil. Working in batches, sauté the bok choy 8–10 minutes, until wilted but not fully cooked. Drain in a colander and let cool.
2. In batches, pulse the bok choy in a food processor 20–25 times, into small pieces but not a purée.
3. Squeeze the chopped bok choy in a nut milk bag or cheesecloth to remove most of the liquid, about 80–85% of it, so the filling isn't wet but stays juicy.
4. Mix the bok choy with the pork, soy sauce, salt, sugar, and white pepper, stirring vigorously until it comes together into a paste. Microwave a teaspoon for 30 seconds and taste; adjust the seasoning before wrapping.
5. To fold: put filling in the center of a wrapper, fold it in half, curl it down halfway, then bring the two bottom corners together, dab one with water, and press to seal.
6. To boil: drop the dumplings into a large pot of rolling-boiling water without crowding. When they float (1–2 minutes), add 1 cup cold water and bring back to a rolling boil, 45–60 seconds. Serve with the dipping sauce or in warm chicken stock with scallions.
7. To pan-fry: drizzle 1 tbsp oil into a 10–11 inch nonstick skillet and arrange the dumplings in circles, not touching. Pour 1 cup cold water around them, cover, and cook over medium-high until most of the water evaporates (6–7 minutes). Lower to medium and cook until the bottoms are golden and crispy.

## Notes

- For the dipping sauce, mix equal parts soy sauce and rice vinegar with chili oil to taste.
- To freeze, lay the dumplings on a parchment-lined sheet pan, not touching, until firm, then bag them; they keep about a month. Boil from frozen without thawing, adding the cup of cold water twice, or pan-fry 2–3 minutes longer.
- You can add up to 2 more pounds of pork for a meatier filling (3:1 bok choy to pork is her preference), or use ground chicken or turkey with a little added oil.
