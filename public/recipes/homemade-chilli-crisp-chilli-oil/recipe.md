## Ingredients

- 400 ml neutral cooking oil with a high smoke point, such as rapeseed
- 2 shallots, thinly sliced
- 10 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
- 40 g Chinese dried chilli flakes
- 15 g medium chilli flakes
- 15 g Korean gochugaru (or more medium chilli flakes)
- 3 cm piece ginger, thinly sliced
- 2 whole star anise
- 1 tbsp Sichuan peppercorns
- 1 cassia cinnamon stick
- 3 tsp granulated sugar
- 2 tsp salt
- 1 tsp MSG (optional)
- 1 tbsp Chinese black vinegar (optional)
- 50 g roasted peanuts, roughly chopped (optional)
- 2 tbsp white sesame seeds (optional)

## Instructions

1. Thinly slice the shallots and garlic with a mandoline or sharp knife. Put the dried chillies in a bowl and set the ginger, star anise, Sichuan peppercorns, and cinnamon stick on a separate plate.
2. Heat the oil in a pot over medium heat and add the shallots and garlic, stirring occasionally. Fry patiently until golden brown, then lift them out with a slotted spoon onto kitchen towel to crisp and drain. Keep the oil.
3. Return the oil to the pot, add the whole spices, and let them sizzle for 3–4 minutes.
4. In a separate bowl, combine the dried chillies, sugar, salt, MSG and black vinegar (if using), and the peanuts and sesame seeds (if using).
5. Turn off the heat and let the oil cool for about 2 minutes, to roughly 180°C/360°F. Pour the hot oil over the chilli mixture, stirring well.
6. Let it sit for at least 24 hours. Store in airtight jars and stir before each use.

## My Notes

- Make it with the black vinegar.

## Notes

- The shallots and garlic burn quickly once they start to colour, so fry them patiently.
- Chinese dried chilli flakes add fragrance and colour more than heat; Sichuan peppercorns give the numbing tingle, and gochugaru a smoky note.
- For less heat, use fewer or milder chilli flakes, or remove the seeds from the dried chillies.
- A little goes a long way. Kept airtight in the fridge it lasts several months, and the flavour keeps developing.
