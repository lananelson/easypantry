## Ingredients

- 6 garlic cloves
- 2 celery stalks
- 1 large yellow onion
- 1 pound green cabbage
- 1 cup dill leaves
- 12 ounces kielbasa
- 1/4 cup extra-virgin olive oil, plus more for drizzling
- Kosher salt
- Freshly ground black pepper
- 3 tablespoons tomato paste
- 2 (14.5-ounce) cans white beans, such as butter, cannellini, or gigante
- Sour cream or plain whole-milk Greek yogurt, for serving

## Instructions

1. Thinly slice the garlic. Trim the celery and slice it crosswise about 1/4 inch thick. Chop the onion. Quarter the cabbage through the root end, trim out the cores, and slice the cabbage crosswise into 1-inch-thick strips. Cut the kielbasa in half lengthwise, then slice crosswise into 1/2-inch-thick half-moons.
2. Heat 1/4 cup olive oil in a large Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Add the kielbasa in a single layer and cook, undisturbed, until golden brown and crisp, 4 to 5 minutes. Stir to redistribute and cook 2 to 3 minutes longer.
3. Add the garlic, celery, and onion, reduce the heat to medium, and cook, stirring occasionally, until the onion is translucent, 4 to 5 minutes.
4. Stir in the cabbage, 2 teaspoons salt, and lots of black pepper. Cook, stirring often, until the onion is very soft and beginning to stick to the pot and the cabbage is just tender but not crunchy, 4 to 5 minutes.
5. Stir in the tomato paste and cook until it starts to stick to the bottom of the pot and sizzles a little louder, about 2 minutes.
6. Meanwhile, drain and rinse the white beans. Stir them into the pot, using a wooden spoon to smash about 1/3 cup of them against the side.
7. Stir in 4 cups water. Bring to a simmer over medium-high heat, then reduce the heat to maintain a simmer and cook for 15 minutes, until slightly thickened and the flavors have melded.
8. Taste and add more salt and black pepper as needed. Chop the dill and stir it into the soup.
9. Ladle into bowls and serve each with a dollop of sour cream, more black pepper, and a drizzle of olive oil.

## Notes

- Smashing some of the beans releases their starch and helps thicken the soup.
- Molly notes that the soup should taste much more developed after the 15-minute simmer; time is an important ingredient here.
- The recipe introduction suggests Italian sausage as another direction when kielbasa is not what you want.
