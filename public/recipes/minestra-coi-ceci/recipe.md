## Ingredients

- 2 1/2 cups (1 pound) dried chickpeas
- 2 teaspoons baking soda
- 3 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
- 1 medium onion, diced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 branches fresh rosemary
- 1 tablespoon tomato paste, dissolved in a little warm water
- Salt
- Freshly ground black pepper
- 4 cups chicken broth, or 4 chicken bouillon cubes dissolved in 4 cups hot water
- 4 quarts water
- 1 tablespoon coarse sea salt
- 12 ounces quadrucci, pastine, or other tiny pasta
- Freshly grated pecorino romano or Parmigiano-Reggiano cheese

## Instructions

1. About 12 hours before making the soup, put the chickpeas in a large bowl and cover them with cold water by 3 inches. Add the baking soda and soak for at least 12 hours.
2. Drain and rinse the chickpeas very well under cold running water. Put them in a 5-quart pot, cover with 3 inches of fresh cold water, bring to a boil, and cook at a steady low simmer until almost soft, 1 1/2 to 2 hours.
3. While the chickpeas cook, warm the olive oil in a sauté pan with the onion, garlic, and rosemary. Sauté over low heat until the garlic is pale golden. Add the dissolved tomato paste, salt, and a little pepper and simmer 10 to 15 minutes more. Discard the rosemary branches.
4. Add the contents of the sauté pan to the chickpeas when about 30 minutes of their cooking time remains.
5. When the chickpeas are cooked, drain them, reserving 1 cup of their cooking liquid. Add the chicken broth to the reserved liquid and mix well, then stir this broth mixture into the chickpeas.
6. Shortly before serving, bring 4 quarts water to a rolling boil, add the coarse salt and pasta, and cook until al dente, about 4 to 6 minutes. Drain and stir the pasta into the chickpea mixture. Taste for salt and cook 2 minutes to incorporate.
7. Serve immediately with grated pecorino cheese.

## Notes

- The source describes this as a nourishing soup almost as thick as a stew.
- The baking soda in the soaking water softens the chickpea skins; the source notes that flour was traditionally used for the same purpose.
