## Ingredients

- 350 g potatoes, peeled weight
- 250 g young chard or other tender greens
- 200 g dried beans
- 150 g pumpkin, peeled weight
- 1 small bunch borage, tender leaves
- 30 g dried porcini mushrooms
- 2 medium onions
- 200 g pasta (scuccuzzù, ditalini, or broken spaghetti)
- 3 heaping tablespoons pesto alla Genovese, preferably without pine nuts
- Salt, to taste

## Instructions

1. Soak the beans in cold water for at least 12 hours. Rinse well under running water, then place them in fresh cold water, bring to a boil, and cook for about 10 minutes. Drain and discard the cooking water.
2. Meanwhile, soak the dried mushrooms. Wash the chard and borage, and clean and chop the onions.
3. Cut the potatoes and pumpkin into cubes and the borage and greens into thin strips.
4. Bring 2.5 liters of water to a boil. Add the vegetables, prepared beans, and chopped soaked mushrooms.
5. Cover and cook very slowly for at least 2 1/2 hours, adding hot water if needed. The soup should simmer gently until the vegetables gradually break down and the broth thickens. Near the end, if needed, raise the heat slightly and mash any remaining intact pieces with the back of a spoon to make the soup creamier.
6. Add the pasta and return the soup to a boil. Season with salt, then turn off the heat. Cover and let stand for the pasta's stated cooking time; for scuccuzzù, the source specifies 15 minutes.
7. Stir in the pesto thoroughly and serve.

## Notes

- The source describes a proper Genovese minestrone as very thick, traditionally dense enough to pass the “spoon test.”
- This is the author's winter version; zucchini, green beans, and eggplant are omitted in favor of pumpkin, greens, and optional dried mushrooms.
- A Parmesan rind is traditionally cooked with the vegetables, although it is discussed in the source rather than included in this version's ingredient list.
- Leftovers are good the next day and, especially in summer, can be eaten cold.
