## Ingredients

- 1/2 lemon
- 4 large egg yolks
- 1 large egg
- 2 ounces grated Parmesan cheese (about 1/2 cup), plus more for serving
- Kosher salt
- 4 ounces cured (Spanish) chorizo (about a 6-inch piece)
- 1 (14.5-ounce) can chickpeas
- 3 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
- Freshly ground black pepper
- 1 pound dried rigatoni

## Instructions

1. Bring a large pot of heavily salted water to a boil.
2. In a medium bowl, whisk together the egg yolks and whole egg. Whisk in the grated Parmesan.
3. Dice the chorizo into 1/4-inch pieces. Drain and rinse the chickpeas in a fine-mesh strainer.
4. In a large Dutch oven, combine the chorizo and olive oil. Cook over medium heat until the chorizo just starts to sizzle, about 3 minutes.
5. Add the chickpeas and stir well to coat in the oil. Continue cooking, stirring occasionally and crushing some of the chickpeas with the back of a wooden spoon, until they are crisp in some areas and the chorizo has released some of its red oil, 5 to 6 minutes longer. Coarsely grind plenty of black pepper into the pot to bloom the pepper and bring out its aroma. Remove the pot from the heat.
6. While the chickpeas cook, add the rigatoni to the boiling water and cook according to the package directions for al dente. Reserve 1 1/2 cups of the pasta water, then drain the pasta.
7. Whisk 1/2 cup of the reserved pasta water into the egg and Parmesan mixture until well combined.
8. Transfer the pasta to the Dutch oven along with about half of the remaining pasta water. Turn the heat to medium-low and stir in the egg mixture, stirring vigorously and constantly, until the pasta is coated in a thick, creamy, pale-yellow sauce. Add a splash or two more pasta water if the sauce looks too thick.
9. Finely grate the zest of the 1/2 lemon into the pasta. Taste and add more salt and black pepper if needed.
10. Top with more grated Parmesan and black pepper and serve.

## Notes

- Molly frames this as a Spanish-leaning carbonara: cured chorizo replaces guanciale, adding smoky flavor and rendered fat, while chickpeas become crisp and add texture.
- Tempering the egg mixture with hot pasta water helps keep the eggs from scrambling when they hit the hot pasta.
