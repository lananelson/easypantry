## Ingredients

- 1 medium green or red cabbage (about 2 pounds)
- 3 garlic cloves
- 1 cup mint leaves
- 1 lemon
- 1 egg
- 1 pound ground lamb
- 5 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 1/3 cup panko bread crumbs
- 1/2 cup plus 2 tablespoons tahini
- 1 1/2 teaspoons ground cumin

## Instructions

1. Position one rack in the upper third of the oven and a second one in the middle. Preheat the oven to 450°F.
2. Quarter the cabbage through the root end. Trim out the core of each quarter, then cut each quarter in half crosswise to create 8 pieces total. Separate the leaves and scatter them across 2 rimmed baking sheets.
3. Drizzle each baking sheet with 2 tablespoons olive oil and season each with 3/4 teaspoon salt and lots of black pepper. Massage the oil into the cabbage so it is evenly distributed. Roast until charred in some places and reduced in volume, 13 to 15 minutes.
4. Remove from the oven and combine all of the cabbage on one baking sheet, reserving the other baking sheet for the meatballs. Increase the oven temperature to 500°F.
5. In a large bowl, lightly beat the egg. Add the panko, 2 tablespoons tahini, ground cumin, 1 1/4 teaspoons salt, and 1 tablespoon olive oil and whisk to combine.
6. Finely grate 2 garlic cloves into the panko mixture. Chop 1/2 cup mint leaves and add them along with about 1/3 pound of the ground lamb. Gently mix until evenly distributed, then mix in the remaining lamb without overworking it.
7. Roll the meatball mixture into 12 equal-size balls about 1 1/2 inches wide. Arrange them on the empty baking sheet.
8. Return both baking sheets to the oven, placing the meatballs on the top rack. Roast until the meatballs are browned and spring back when pressed gently and the cabbage is deeply charred and crisp, 7 to 9 minutes. If the cabbage needs more time, leave it in the oven after pulling the meatballs.
9. While the meatballs roast, stir together 1/2 cup tahini, 1/3 cup water, the juice of 1 lemon, and 3/4 teaspoon salt in a small bowl. Finely grate the remaining garlic clove into the tahini. Whisk to combine; add another splash of water if needed to make the sauce pourable.
10. Spread some of the tahini sauce on each plate and top with the cabbage, meatballs, and remaining 1/2 cup mint leaves.

## Notes

- Molly says the cabbage is as much the star as the meatballs: roasting it until the moisture is driven off makes it tender, deeply charred, and crisp.
- The meatballs stay juicy because tahini and olive oil are mixed directly into them.
- Start by mixing only a small amount of lamb into the panko mixture so the seasonings distribute evenly, then add the rest. Avoid overworking the mixture or the meatballs can become tough.
- Keeping the meatballs on the upper rack avoids having another pan directly above them, which would trap steam and inhibit browning.
