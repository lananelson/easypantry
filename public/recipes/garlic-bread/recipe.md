## Ingredients

- 3 cups (24 ounces) extra-virgin olive oil
- 40 garlic cloves, peeled
- 1 pound (4 sticks) unsalted butter, at room temperature
- 3 ounces sharp pecorino (preferably Locatelli), grated
- 1 tablespoon dried Sicilian oregano
- 2 teaspoons kosher salt
- 1 tablespoon freshly ground black pepper
- 2 semolina bastone (Italian bread, or French baguette)

## Instructions

1. In a small saucepan over low heat, add the olive oil and half of the garlic. Cook, stirring occasionally, until the garlic is soft and spreadable, 45 minutes to 1 hour.
2. Let cool slightly, then strain the garlic-flavored oil into a jar. Set the cooked garlic aside to cool to room temperature.
3. Finely chop 2 ounces of the remaining garlic cloves. Coat a small sauté pan generously with some of the garlic oil and bring it close to the smoke point. Add the chopped garlic and cook until browned and crispy. Remove from the pan and add to the cooled garlic confit.
4. Finely chop the remaining garlic cloves and add them to the fried and confit garlic.
5. Preheat the oven to 425°F.
6. Add the butter to a bowl and stir in the garlic mixture, pecorino, Sicilian oregano, salt, and black pepper until incorporated.
7. Split the loaves in half lengthwise and spread the garlic butter generously from coast to coast. Close the loaves, wrap in foil, and bake for 10 minutes.
8. Open the foil and open the bread butter-side up, leaving it on the foil. Return to the oven for about 10 minutes, until the edges are golden brown and crispy, almost to the point of being burnt.

## Notes

- Christian Petroni describes this as his version of an ultra-garlicky Italian-American garlic bread, inspired by the garlic bread at The Venice.
- The double-bake is key: the first bake lets the butter permeate the loaf, and the second crisps the crust.
