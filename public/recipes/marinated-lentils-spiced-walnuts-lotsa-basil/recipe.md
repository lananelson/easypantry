## Ingredients

- 3 garlic cloves
- 1 1/2 cups packed basil leaves
- 1 (4-ounce) block feta cheese
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 1 1/2 cups black beluga lentils or French green lentils (a.k.a. lentilles du Puy)
- 1 cup raw walnuts, almonds, or pecans
- 1/2 cup extra-virgin olive oil, plus more for drizzling
- 1 tablespoon cumin seeds
- 1 1/2 teaspoons ground coriander
- 1/3 cup golden raisins
- 1/3 cup red or white wine vinegar

## Instructions

1. Bring a medium saucepan filled with 2 quarts water and 2 tablespoons salt to a boil.
2. Add the lentils, stir, and return the water to a simmer, adjusting the heat as necessary to maintain a simmer. Cook until the lentils are cooked through and no longer toothsome but not mushy, 15 to 35 minutes depending on the type and age of the lentils. Taste periodically to evaluate doneness before draining.
3. Drain the lentils in a fine-mesh strainer and let them cool in the strainer while you prepare the walnut dressing. Reserve the saucepan.
4. Coarsely chop the walnuts.
5. In the reserved saucepan, combine 1/2 cup olive oil and the chopped walnuts. Cook over medium-low heat, swirling the pan often so the walnuts cook evenly, until golden brown, 4 to 6 minutes. Remove from the heat.
6. Add the cumin seeds and ground coriander and stir to combine. Immediately grate the garlic cloves into the still-hot oil and stir to combine.
7. Stir in the golden raisins, red wine vinegar, and 1 teaspoon salt.
8. Add the drained lentils back to the saucepan with the walnut mixture and gently stir everything together.
9. Thinly slice the feta into large 1/4-inch-thick planks.
10. Tear the basil leaves in half, or into small pieces if large, and toss them through the lentils. Taste and adjust the seasoning as needed.
11. Divide the lentils among 4 serving bowls. Top each with a few feta planks and black pepper, and drizzle with more olive oil.

## Notes

- Molly notes that lentils are especially good for marinating because they absorb flavor readily and often taste even better after sitting for a few days.
- Black or green lentils are used because both tend to hold their shape well when cooked, making them good for salads.
- The residual heat from the walnut oil toasts the cumin and coriander and mellows the raw garlic.
