## Ingredients

- 2 liters of water
- 4 stalks of lemongrass
- 1 inch chunk of galangal
- 10 kaffir lime leaves
- 10 Thai chilies
- 5 cloves of garlic
- ½ kilo shrimp
- 300 grams of oyster mushrooms
- 2 roma tomatoes
- 2 white onions (medium sized)
- 2 teaspoons of sugar
- 8 - 12 tablespoons of fish sauce (depending on your taste)
- 8 - 12 tablespoons of lime juice (10 - 15 limes)
- Handful of cilantro
- 3 tablespoons canned Thai roasted chili sauce, nam prik pao น้ำพริกเผา (creamy version)
- 10 tablespoons evaporated milk (creamy version)

## Instructions

1. Put about 2 liters of water in a pot to boil.
2. Squeeze the limes now so they're ready later: slice about 10 limes and juice them into a small bowl, and set aside.
3. Tear off the outermost leaf of each lemongrass stalk and discard. Lightly pound the lemongrass with a mallet to release the flavors, then slice it diagonally into 1 inch strips or so.
4. Take about 1 thumb sized chunk of the root part of galangal, and chop it into slices.
5. Coarsely tear about 10 kaffir lime leaves to help release their flavor.
6. Peel about 5 cloves of garlic.
7. Stem the Thai bird chilies (prik kee noo), then either slice them in two or give them a little pound on your cutting board (careful of flying seeds). Remove the seeds for less heat.
8. Throw the lemongrass, galangal, kaffir lime leaves, garlic and chilies into the water. Put on the lid so it boils and releases the herb flavors quicker.
9. Prepare the shrimp (for freshwater shrimp, chop off the tentacles).
10. Boil the soup with the herbs for about 10 minutes, then toss in the shrimp and turn the fire to low.
11. Add the mushrooms, rinsed beforehand.
12. Slice the tomatoes and onions into big, chunky wedges and add them.
13. Skim off any foam that builds up on top.
14. Boil for about 2 - 3 minutes.
15. Add about 6 tablespoons of fish sauce and 2 teaspoons of sugar to start; you may need more of each.
16. Boil for another minute or so and turn off the heat. Don't overcook: the mushrooms and onions should just be soft.
17. With the heat off, add about 8 tablespoons of lime juice (depending on how sour you like it).
18. Taste until perfectly sour and salty, adding more fish sauce, sugar, or lime juice as needed.
19. Chop a handful of cilantro, throw it in, and give a final stir. The lime juice and cilantro taste fresher when not boiled. This is tom yum goong nam sai (ต้มยำกุ้งน้ำใส), the clear version.
20. For the creamy version: set aside about half the soup as the clear version. Turn the heat back on low under the rest. Stir in about 3 heaping tablespoons of nam prik pao and about 10 tablespoons of evaporated milk, and heat for about 1 minute.
21. Taste again: the chili paste and milk change the balance, so you may need more lime juice and fish sauce.
