## Ingredients

- 2 lemons
- 1 garlic clove
- 1 cup basil leaves
- 1 (1 1/2-ounce) piece Parmesan cheese (about 1/3 cup grated)
- 12 ounces fresh buffalo mozzarella or burrata cheese
- 8 ounces sliced mortadella, soppressata, or prosciutto
- Kosher salt
- 1 1/2 cups Castelvetrano olives
- Freshly ground black pepper
- 2/3 cup extra-virgin olive oil
- 1 pound dried rigatoni
- 2/3 cup roasted, salted pistachios

## Instructions

1. Bring a large pot of heavily salted water to a boil; the recipe calls for a full 1/4 cup salt.
2. Make the olive-y dressing: Firmly smash the Castelvetrano olives with the bottom of a glass measuring cup to release their pits. Discard the pits, tear the olive flesh in half, and transfer to a large bowl.
3. Finely grate the zest of half of 1 lemon into the bowl. Cut both lemons in half and squeeze the juice into the bowl, catching the seeds with your hands.
4. Using a Microplane, finely grate the garlic and Parmesan into the bowl.
5. Stir in the olive oil.
6. Tear the mozzarella and mortadella into bite-size pieces and add them to the dressing, tossing gently to combine. Season with salt and lots of black pepper.
7. Cook the pasta: Add the rigatoni to the boiling water and stir well. Cook according to the package directions until al dente, then drain and rinse in cold water to cool the pasta to room temperature and rinse off excess starch. Drain well.
8. Assemble and serve: Add the pasta to the bowl of dressing. Chop the pistachios and tear the basil leaves; add them to the salad and gently toss everything to combine. Taste and add more salt and black pepper as needed. Serve at room temperature or cold.

## Notes

- Molly argues that pasta salad works best with an oil-and-acid dressing rather than mayonnaise, because the dressing soaks into the cooked pasta and improves as it sits instead of congealing.
- Because this is a cold pasta dish, rinse the cooked rigatoni to remove excess starch rather than saving that starch to thicken a sauce.
- If not serving immediately, wait to add the pistachios so they do not get soggy, and wait to add the basil so it does not wilt.
