## Ingredients

- 2 cups (1 pound) dried borlotti or cranberry beans, or 1 (19-ounce) can Italian borlotti or cranberry beans, undrained (for bean sauce)
- 10 cups water (for bean sauce)
- 4 tablespoons unsalted butter (for bean sauce)
- 1 small white onion, sliced extremely fine (for bean sauce)
- 1/3 cup (2 ounces) minced pancetta, salt pork, or bacon (for bean sauce)
- 1 cup meat broth, or 1 meat bouillon cube dissolved in 1 cup hot water (for bean sauce)
- 2 teaspoons tomato paste, just enough to add color (for bean sauce)
- 8 leaves fresh basil (for bean sauce)
- 2 European bay leaves, preferably fresh; use only 1 bay leaf if using dried California bay (for bean sauce)
- Substantial grindings of fresh black pepper (for bean sauce)
- 1/2 scant cup fine dry bread crumbs (for pisarei)
- About 3/4 cup very hot water (for pisarei)
- 2 cups (10 ounces) unbleached all-purpose flour (for pisarei)
- 1 egg (for pisarei)
- At least 4 quarts water (for cooking)
- 2 teaspoons salt (for cooking)

## Instructions

1. If using dried beans, soak them 12 hours in enough water to cover by 2 to 3 inches. Drain and rinse well. Put them in a 4- or 5-quart heavy-bottomed pot, cover with 10 cups water, and simmer slowly for about 2 hours, or until tender. Drain, reserving the cooking water. If using canned beans, drain them and reserve their liquid.
2. Melt the butter in a large heavy pot and add the onion. Cook over low heat until pale and limp, about 15 minutes. Add the pancetta and cook another 5 to 6 minutes.
3. Add the beans, 1 1/2 cups reserved bean liquid, meat broth, tomato paste, basil, bay leaf or leaves, and plenty of pepper. Cook over medium heat for 4 to 5 minutes. If too thick, add more bean liquid or broth. Discard the bay leaves.
4. While the beans cook, make the pisarei. Put the bread crumbs in a bowl and add enough hot water to soften them, mixing well. Put all but 2 tablespoons of the flour in a large bowl, make a well, and place the moistened bread crumbs in it. Mix in the egg and enough very hot water to make a firm but elastic dough.
5. Knead on a lightly floured surface for 10 to 12 minutes, adding small amounts of flour as necessary, until firm and silky. Alternatively, mix in a heavy-duty electric pasta mixer for about 5 minutes. Cover and rest 30 minutes to 1 hour.
6. Divide the dough into 14 pieces, keeping unused pieces covered. Cut each piece in half and roll with your palms into a pencil-thin log, about 3/8 inch in diameter. Cut into pieces about 1/2 inch long. Press each firmly against the work surface with your thumb to form little gnocchi, about the size of the borlotti beans.
7. To serve immediately, cook the pisarei in at least 4 quarts boiling salted water for about 7 to 15 minutes, depending on freshness, until tender. Drain and toss with the borlotti bean sauce.

## Notes

- The source describes pisarei as a traditional Piacenza pasta made with bread crumbs and flour.
- The bean sauce can be made 2 days ahead and refrigerated. The pasta can be made and shaped 1 day ahead. Leftovers reheat well.
- Shaped pisarei can be held on a parchment-lined baking sheet, sprinkled with semolina or a granulated flour such as Wondra, covered, and refrigerated until later the same day.
- Serve Parmigiano-Reggiano at the table.
