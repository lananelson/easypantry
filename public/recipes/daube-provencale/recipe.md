## Ingredients

- 3 lb stew beef, cut into 1 inch cubes
- 1 (750-ml) bottle Cote du Rhone (or Vacqueyras, Gigondas, or Minervois; Bordeaux also works)
- salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 3 sprigs fresh thyme
- 2 bay leaves
- 3 carrots, peeled and cut into 1/2 inch slices
- 1 orange, washed well and cut into 8 wedges (or grapefruit, see notes)
- 1 large onion, peeled and cut into rings
- 2 cloves garlic, peeled and sliced
- olive oil
- 1 6-oz can tomato paste
- 4 1/2 cups water
- 3 1/2 oz. pitted black olives

## Instructions

1. Season meat with salt and pepper on all sides. Place meat in a big bowl with wine, thyme, bay leaves, carrots, orange, onion, and garlic. Cover and marinate in the fridge for at least 12 hours, and preferably 24 hours.
2. Heat some olive oil over medium heat in a large stockpot. Remove the meat from the marinade and brown the pieces in the oil on all sides. Pour the marinade (including the oranges, bay leaves, etc.) over the meat and bring to a boil. Boil for 5 minutes, skimming any foam from the surface. Lower the heat to a simmer, stir in the tomato paste, and add 4 1/2 cups water. Cover, and let stew on low heat for 2-3 hours, stirring occasionally.
3. 15 minutes before the stew is ready, add the pitted olives. Let cook for 15 minutes on medium heat without the lid to thicken the stew.
4. Serve over mashed potatoes, rice, or pasta.

## Notes

- From the blogger who adapted it: using a whole grapefruit instead of the orange gave the stew a slightly bitter aftertaste. Instead, add the grapefruit zest and fruit separately, avoiding the bitter pith. The same technique could be applied to oranges.
